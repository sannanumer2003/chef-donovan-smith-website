The Art of the Intimate Dinner

By Chef Donovan Smith June 2026

What separates a good dinner from one your guests talk about for years? It starts long before the first course arrives. It starts with intention — the kind that moves through every decision made in the kitchen, at the market, and at the table itself.

For over eleven years, Chef Donovan Smith has hosted hundreds of private dinners across The Bahamas and beyond. And the throughline in every memorable evening is the same: the space created for the guest to simply be present.

The Silence Before the First Course

There is a moment — just before the first plate is set down — where the room holds its breath. The candles are lit. The table is dressed. The guests are seated. That moment is not accidental. It is the product of hours of quiet, deliberate preparation.

At a private dinner for six in Nassau last spring, Chef Donovan and his team spent two hours arranging the outdoor terrace overlooking the water. Not because six people required two hours of setup — but because the goal was not a table. The goal was a feeling.

“When someone sits down at my table, I want them to forget, just for a few hours, everything outside of it.”

That feeling — of ease, of arrival, of being genuinely cared for — is what distinguishes a private dining experience from simply eating well.

Food as Conversation

The menu for an intimate dinner is never arbitrary. Each course is chosen not just for flavour, but for the conversation it invites. A light citrus crudo slows the pace down. A rich braised short rib deepens the mood. The sequence matters as much as the ingredients.

Chef Donovan approaches every private menu as a narrative — with a beginning that opens, a middle that builds, and a close that lingers. The final bite should feel like the last line of a sentence worth reading twice.

The People at the Table

Ultimately, the table is secondary to the people around it. The finest ingredients and the most considered mise en place mean nothing if the guests do not feel at home. That is why Chef Donovan and his team approach every event with the same hospitality that defines the best hotels in the world — attentive without being intrusive, present without being visible.

An intimate dinner is a gift. Whether for two on an anniversary, or twelve at a milestone celebration, the best ones leave guests feeling not just fed, but remembered.
